## Background

As you read this, millions of AI chips in the San Francisco Bay Area are running at full speed, serving AI to people around the world.

Most of those chips come from Nvidia, the world's most important supplier of AI computing power and once the most valuable company on Earth.

Jensen Huang is the founder and helmsman of that company. Over 33 years of service, his vision and persistence made an indelible contribution to Nvidia's growth.[^2]

When Nvidia was founded, its main business was gaming graphics cards, and few people thought much of that trade. The company came close to bankruptcy several times, but Huang held on.

Nvidia bet on AI computing chips and the CUDA ecosystem some twenty years ago (the foundation of its present dominance in AI computing supply), and that too came from Huang's technical insight and far-sighted decisions.[^1]

Until a few days ago, Huang was still hands-on: he took direct reports from more than 60 executives, managed relations with suppliers, customers and governments, and was the soul of the company and one of the leaders of the global AI industry.[^1]

Yet a man of such standing and such contributions has just resigned over an extramarital affair scandal.[^2][^3] The ending is not only regrettable but also puzzling.

After all, other founders of American tech giants (Amazon's Jeff Bezos, Microsoft's Bill Gates, Google's Sergey Brin) have had affair controversies of their own, including affairs with subordinates that could raise conflicts of interest, and the corporate fallout was very limited.[^4][^6][^7] Bezos and Brin in particular both kept control of their companies and faced no discipline from their boards.[^4][^5][^8]

Why was Huang the only one to resign over an affair scandal, barely two weeks after it broke?[^2][^40] Is there a deeper reason behind his resignation?

To find the answers, I put together a timeline of what happened:

## Timeline

*Undercurrents*

2025-01-20 Trump is inaugurated as the 47th President of the United States.

2025-04-09 The US government requires a Commerce Department license for exports of Nvidia's H20 chips to China. The same month, Nvidia announces a large-scale plan to build AI infrastructure in the United States.[^11]

2025-07-14 Nvidia announces that the government has assured it licenses will be granted and that H20 sales to China will resume. Trump and Huang had met at the White House the week before.[^12][^13]

2025-10-28 Nvidia holds its GTC conference in Washington. Huang gives the keynote and publicly praises Trump's policies.[^14]

2025-12-08 Trump approves sales of Nvidia's more advanced H200 chips to China, with the government taking a 25% cut.[^15]

2026-01-13 The Commerce Department issues a new rule requiring case-by-case review of H200 exports to China. The next day, Chinese customs says H200 chips are not permitted to enter the country.[^16][^17]

2026-01-21 to 2026-04-22 Both parties in Congress push legislation to restrict chip exports. The AI Overwatch Act would give Congress the power to review and block exports of advanced chips.[^18][^19] The Chip Security Act would require advanced chips to carry location verification,[^20] and the MATCH Act would press US allies to restrict exports of semiconductor manufacturing equipment, such as lithography machines, to China.[^21] The Trump administration and Nvidia oppose these bills, and before the Huang scandal broke none of them had passed the full House or Senate.[^22]

2026-05-13 Trump visits China. Huang joins the delegation at the last minute, hoping to break the deadlock over H200 sales. The US issues export licenses, but no chips are actually shipped.[^17][^23][^24]

*The climax*

2026-10-29 The New York Times (a pro-Democratic outlet) first reports that Huang is seen as the target of a Chinese spy operation, saying that Alice Huang (Huang Le), who was close to him, passed information about him to the Chinese government. The sources are "US intelligence sources." Huang and Nvidia deny that the two had any unusual relationship.[^40]

2026-10-30 China's Foreign Ministry and its embassy in the US deny the allegations.[^41]

2026-10-31 Trump voices support for Huang for the first time.[^42]

2026-11-03 US midterm elections. The Republican Party led by Trump suffers a heavy defeat, and Democrats win control of both chambers.[^25][^26]

2026-11-04 Three House committee chairmen jointly write to Huang, asking him to attend a closed-door session on November 18.[^27][^28] All three are senior Republicans. Two of them (Mast and Moolenaar) have long advocated restricting chip exports to China, and the third (Crawford) is responsible for counterintelligence work. Because of the midterm defeat, their chairmanships will end in early 2027, and any bill left unfinished when this Congress ends will die. For Mast and Moolenaar, the remaining two months are the last chance to pass China chip export restrictions during their tenure.

2026-11-06 A follow-up report in The New York Times says a memo circulated among senior US officials concluded that Alice Huang was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security, citing unnamed people briefed on the intelligence. The Financial Times reports the same day that US officials have concluded the two had a romantic relationship.[^43][^44]

2026-11-08 Huang sends an internal email admitting he was unfaithful to his wife and saying he is willing to take responsibility and cooperate with an investigation. The same day, Nvidia's board announces an investigation into Huang's personal conduct and says he will remain CEO until it is complete.[^45][^46][^29] Trump publicly defends Huang.[^3] The New York Times reports that Trump was briefed on the matter in October 2025 but took no action.[^47][^30]

2026-11-09 Trump confirms that the FBI is involved in the investigation.[^48] The House Democratic leader demands answers from Nvidia over the alleged threat to national security and calls for restrictions on chip exports to China.[^49]

2026-11-11 The New York Times reports a second affair, between Huang and a female subordinate.[^50][^31] The same day, The Wall Street Journal (whose news reporting is well regarded in the industry, but which was sued by Trump in 2025 and is on bad terms with him) reports that Nvidia's board is preparing to remove Huang. The board meets that evening to discuss his future.[^51][^32] At this point the board has not announced that its investigation is complete, nor disclosed any findings.

2026-11-12 Nvidia's board announces that Huang has resigned from all his positions at the company, including president, CEO and director.[^2][^3] Yet on the same day, the US Attorney General says at a press conference that no conclusion has been reached on whether Alice Huang is a Chinese intelligence operative.[^34]

2026-11-13 The Wall Street Journal reports that Colette Kress, Nvidia's new interim CEO, had questioned some of Huang's deals, which caused a rift between them.[^52] Reuters reports that House Speaker Johnson says a consensus is developing in Congress to further tighten exports of advanced chips to China.[^35]

2026-11-14 People from many quarters rally behind Huang. Elon Musk and others sign a volunteer-organized open letter asking Nvidia's board to reinstate Huang unless there is solid evidence that his staying on would harm the company, and to give him a fair chance to respond. According to figures published by the organizers, the letter has been signed by more than 7,200 current employees (about 17% of the workforce) and more than 70,000 shareholders.[^36][^37][^38]

## Aftermath

1. Congress will speed up legislation to tighten exports of chips and semiconductor technology to China, and the work will very likely be finished before the end of the year.[^35][^39] This will increase pressure on China's supply of advanced AI chips in the short term, but it will also push China to accelerate domestic substitution of computing power and to remove American components from its high-end supply chain.
2. It will reinforce the consensus that commercial interests must give way to nationalist political positions. Rational moderates will have less of a voice, and the Trump administration will have less room to maneuver in its dealings with China.
3. It will deepen the distrust that elite ethnic Chinese feel toward the United States and accelerate the return of Silicon Valley talent.

## My view

As I see it, the scandal Huang fell into is at bottom a case of his weakness being exploited by Republican China hawks, Democrats and the major media to serve political ends. In their eyes, Huang has three "sins":

First, he followed the wrong people and chose the wrong side. After Trump's midterm defeat, some Republicans cut ties with him and Democrats piled on, and Huang, as Trump's ally, became a target for retribution.

Second, as someone of Chinese descent, he leads a core supplier of critical infrastructure in the United States, which arouses the distrust and resentment of Washington's white political elite.

Third, he supports and promotes the export of advanced AI computing power to China on the grounds of commercial interest. With an anti-China consensus forming in Congress, that position stands out all the more.

So even though Huang built Nvidia with his own hands, his legitimacy to keep running it no longer exists in the eyes of these political elites. They need Nvidia to install a leader who shares their political position. If Huang could be sacrificed to attack the Trump administration and to buy a consensus in Congress for further restrictions on chip exports to China, so much the better.

And so Huang was driven from his post, a casualty of America's partisan struggle and of the geopolitical conflict between China and the United States.

However the story ends, Huang's contributions to the development of humanity's AI industry cannot be erased, the march toward domestic computing power will not stop, and America's open scheme to contain China's technology industry will not succeed.

History will, in the end, deliver a fair verdict for everyone.
